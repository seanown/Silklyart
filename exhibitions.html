Curatorial Program

Exhibitions

SILKLY ART is a standing curatorial house. We curate at our Macau gallery space; mid-to-large thematic exhibitions are co-presented with the government, universities, and cultural institutions. Every exhibition has a theme; every work has a physical showing — that is our fundamental difference from pure e-commerce.

How we curate

From submission to exhibition, five steps

SubmitArtist submits a proposal
ReviewCurator evaluates cultural narrative & craft
ApproveAdmitted to a series — not open to all
ReleaseSilk edition, numbered piece by piece
ExhibitPhysical show, artist on-site talk

Selectivity is the lifeblood of the brand. If open submission floods us with low-quality work, the brand is diluted; therefore “accept” is curatorial gatekeeping, not an open inbox.

Exhibition formats

Two scales,
one curatorial standard

A standing small show runs in our Macau gallery space, keeping works continuously in public view; mid-to-large thematic exhibitions are co-presented with government departments, universities, and cultural institutions, expanding reach and collector contact.

Standing

Macau gallery space

Small standing shows, works rotating continuously; visitors see silk colour and numbering up close.

Co-presented

Mid-to-large thematic

Co-presented with government, universities, and cultural institutions; one cultural theme each time.

On-site

Artist talks

Invite our approved artists for live guided tours, telling the cultural story to the audience.

Principle

Every piece exhibited

A spec iron rule: every released work has a physical exhibition record, becoming part of provenance.

Curatorial directions

Themes we are brewing

The following are curatorial directions (not confirmed dates). Our habit: most works are already catalogued and pre-sold before the show; the exhibition itself is the value-and-news event where artists, buyers, government, and the public gather — building a public market and cultural voice for the silk poster.

Heritage

Dunhuang: origin of the Silk Road

The flying-apsara motifs of the Mogao caves, presented in a way the West can read as Chinese culture.

Heritage

Forbidden City: the archive

Reimagined Forbidden City motifs, split into limited and open tracks.

Living

Nantianmen

Chinese deities and mythological figures (e.g. Mazu, Nezha), created by artists upon licensor approval.

Heritage

Palace Culture

Forbidden City architectural aesthetics and festive motifs — among the strongest single cultural narratives on the site.

Cross-over

Sci-Fi · Film · Music

Same co-creation flow, different licensor, open to contemporary pop culture.

Open

Artist Icons

Invite iconic Chinese and international artists to re-express their vocabulary through the silk poster.

Collaborate

Want to co-present an exhibition?

Government departments, universities, cultural institutions, and venues are welcome to propose co-curated exhibitions.
